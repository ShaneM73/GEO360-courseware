What the equation is saying

Live calculation

d = [(120 m)(65.00 mm)] / (1500 m) = 5.20 mm
Every value is shown with a unit so students can do dimensional analysis directly: (m × mm) / m = mm. Use the same units for h and H. Then d has the same photo unit as r. Here r = P→T, the radial distance to the top image.

Image interpretation

P→B = r − d = 59.80 mm
Consistent notation: r means principal point to top image, and d means relief displacement from base image to top image. So the base-image radius is simply r − d.

3D ground position

R ≈ 590.1 m
R is the true ground radial distance from nadir to the object base. It is held fixed when h or H changes, while the photo distances r and d respond through central projection.
↑ h → ↑ dAt fixed ground position R and H, the base image stays fixed while the top moves outward.
↑ r → ↑ dMoving the object farther from the nadir places its image farther from P and increases relief displacement. Changing direction only rotates the radial line; it does not change the size of d.
↑ H → inwardAt fixed R, both base and top images move toward P, and their separation d becomes smaller.

Detailed derivation of relief displacement

Symbols: r = P→T, d = B→T, P→B = r − d

Notation first, so nothing is ambiguous: r is the radial distance from principal point P to the top image T. d is the relief displacement, the distance from the base image B to the top image T. Therefore the base-image radius is P→B = r − d. In the 3D scene, R is the true ground radial distance from nadir to the object base.

(P→B)/f = R/H  ⇒  P→B = fR/H

Look at the triangle made by the camera center, the photo plane, and the object base. Because the photograph is a central projection, the small image triangle and the large ground triangle are similar. So the image distance to the base is proportional to the ground distance R, with scale factor f/H.

r/f = R/(H − h)  ⇒  r = fR/(H − h)

Now use the triangle for the object top. The top is closer to the camera by height h, so the relevant distance is H − h rather than H. Because of that, the top image plots farther from the principal point than the base image does.

d = r − (P→B) = fR/(H − h) − fR/H

By definition, relief displacement is the separation between the top image and the base image on the photograph. So subtract the base-image radius from the top-image radius.

d = fR[(1/(H − h)) − (1/H)] = fRh / [H(H − h)]

Combine the two fractions. Up to this point the formula is written in terms of f and R, which come from the central-projection geometry.

r = fR/(H − h)  ⇒  fR = r(H − h)

This is the key substitution. Since we defined r as the top-image radial distance, we can solve the previous top-image equation for fR.

d = [r(H − h)]h / [H(H − h)] = hr/H

Substitute fR = r(H − h) into the previous expression. The factor (H − h) cancels, leaving the standard photogrammetry result d = hr/H.

Final: d = hr/H, and P→B = r − d

Interpretation: if h increases, relief displacement increases. If r increases, displacement also increases. If H increases, displacement decreases. And once you know r and d, the base-image radius is just r − d.

Why are there equations on this page? Because the picture alone shows the effect, but the equations explain why the effect happens. They connect the 2D photo panel and the 3D central-projection scene through similar triangles. Also, to be explicit: d is the relief displacement, not r. The symbol r is the radial distance from the principal point to the top image.
