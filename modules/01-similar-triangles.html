How do we prove triangles are similar?

First identify corresponding angles and sides. Then use the smallest amount of information that guarantees the same shape.

△ABC ∼ △DEF

Part 1: Become an angle detective

Use these clues to prove that two angles have equal measures.

Say “equal angles” or “congruent angles.” Their positions may differ, but their degree measures are equal.

1. Given markings

Matching arcs or matching degree labels mean equal angles.

Look first. One arc matches one arc; two arcs match two arcs. Do not match angles merely because they look alike.

2. Vertical angles

Opposite angles formed by two intersecting lines are equal.

If two lines cross like an X, the angles directly across from each other are vertical angles. They are always congruent.

3. Parallel lines

A transversal creates equal corresponding and alternate-interior angles.

Confirm the lines are marked parallel. Then use the familiar F-shape (corresponding) or Z-shape (alternate interior).

4. Triangle angle sum

The three interior angles of every triangle add to 180°.

Find a missing angle with 180° − angle 1 − angle 2. Equal calculated results give another matching pair.

Reliable order:read markings→use angle facts→name corresponding vertices→choose AA, SAS, or SSS

Part 2: Choose AA, SAS, or SSS

You do not need to prove every angle and every side. Any one of these three tests is enough.

Order is evidence: △ABC ∼ △DEF says A ↔ D, B ↔ E, and C ↔ F.
AATwo corresponding angles are equal.
SASTwo side ratios match, and their included angles are equal.
SSSAll three corresponding side ratios match.
